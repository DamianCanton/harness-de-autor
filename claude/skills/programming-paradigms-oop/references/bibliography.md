# Bibliography — programming-paradigms-oop

Canonical sources (no vault material for this subject, spec S9).
Status: [verified] = citation checked online on 2026-11-19; [known] = standard citation, not re-checked online.

## Encapsulation and abstraction
- [known] Parnas, D. L. (1972). On the Criteria to Be Used in Decomposing Systems into Modules. *Communications of the ACM*, 15(12), 1053–1058. Information hiding: hide each design decision likely to change.
- [known] Meyer, B. (1997). *Object-Oriented Software Construction* (2nd ed.). Prentice Hall. Design by Contract, class invariants, Open-Closed Principle (original formulation).

## Substitutability and inheritance
- [verified] Liskov, B. H., & Wing, J. M. (1994). A Behavioral Notion of Subtyping. *ACM Transactions on Programming Languages and Systems*, 16(6), 1811–1841. DOI 10.1145/197320.197383. Subtype must preserve supertype properties (basis of LSP).
- [known] Gamma, E., Helm, R., Johnson, R., & Vlissides, J. (1994). *Design Patterns: Elements of Reusable Object-Oriented Software*. Addison-Wesley. Ch. 1: "Favor object composition over class inheritance"; "Program to an interface, not an implementation".

## SOLID
- [verified] Martin, R. C. (2003). *Agile Software Development, Principles, Patterns, and Practices*. Prentice Hall (released Oct 2002, © 2003). ISBN 0-13-597444-5. Presents SRP, OCP, LSP, ISP, DIP. (Whether this book coined the acronym "SOLID" was not confirmed.)

## Paradigms and side effects
- [verified] Backus, J. (1978). Can Programming Be Liberated from the von Neumann Style? A Functional Style and Its Algebra of Programs. *Communications of the ACM*, 21(8), 613–641. Critique of state-based imperative style; functional combining forms.
- [verified] Hughes, J. (1989). Why Functional Programming Matters. *The Computer Journal*, 32(2), 98–107. DOI 10.1093/comjnl/32.2.98. Higher-order functions and lazy evaluation as modularity tools.
- [verified] Kowalski, R. (1979). Algorithm = Logic + Control. *Communications of the ACM*, 22(7), 424–436. DOI 10.1145/359131.359136. Logic programming: separate what (logic) from how (control).
- [known] Abelson, H., & Sussman, G. J., with Sussman, J. (1996). *Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs* (2nd ed.). MIT Press. Assignment, state, and the cost of mutation (Ch. 3).
